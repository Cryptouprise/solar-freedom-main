## What was actually repaired

This removes assumed savings, blanket cancellation rights, generic legal deadlines and Texas complaint graphics from the selected city experience. Existing distinct city hero assets are retained. The guide explains that a complaint does not automatically end a contract or a separate financing agreement. The FTC discusses the different obligations associated with purchases, leases and PPAs. FAQPage data contains the same three question-and-answer pairs as the visible page. Duplicate server/client FAQ data is removed for these guides. A hidden homepage H1 was also found outside the SPA root and removed only from selected guide responses. Canonicals remain each city’s existing URL. Source checks are dated, not represented as ongoing live legal verification.

Blog Studio’s score no longer counts success messages or adds word-count and density bonuses. It uses a fixed 100-point draft checklist and shows failed or unmeasured criteria without awarding points. Six successful checks with a missing body keyword now score 75 rather than an invented 100. Supplied draft metadata and target phrases are separate from the optional meta-keywords audit. The UI labels the result a draft checklist and discards stale results after edits. It does not claim the published page, indexing or rankings were verified.

## Las Vegas: a shorter path to appointments

Las Vegas and North Las Vegas now display **Choose a call time** beside intake. It opens the existing GHL calendar directly, so homeowners do not have to finish the multi-step city form just to see the booking calendar. The homeowner must still choose a slot and finish GHL’s confirmation process. Existing intake remains available for visitors who prefer to submit details first.

Field-team QR links use `utm_source=door_knocking`, `utm_medium=offline` and `utm_campaign=las_vegas_field_team`. They jump to the city’s booking/intake section without scrolling through the whole guide. The booking-opening event is explicitly `booking_calendar_open`, never an appointment-booked event. Only clean campaign codes are passed in the new direct booking URL, not names, email addresses or phone numbers.

Submitted city forms now supply the existing anonymous journey-session identifier so the backend can link the durable lead to source/medium/campaign records. Their full source URL also retains the campaign query. Rep-code attribution is in that URL, not a new dedicated journey-table field. A visitor using direct GHL booking does not automatically create a first-party city-form lead.

The GHL calendar URL returned HTTP 200. No real lead or appointment was created by verification. Provider-confirmed appointments, attendance and qualified case reviews remain different stages. The Outcomes appointment feed is not yet verified end-to-end: use GHL confirmation records for booking counts until that reconciliation works. Calendar availability, assigned staff, reminders and provider-side campaign persistence were not proved by an HTTP response.

The booking iframe was also opened in Chromium and its visible form loaded. It currently displays **ESR-Team, 30 min**, followed by “Enter details” and “Continue.” The page’s unsupported 15-minute promise was removed. Calendar settings were not changed. A real booking and its assigned staff, available slots, confirmation delivery and attribution were not tested.

For October 3–30, 2026, the live Search Console page-row query returned **97 impressions and 2 clicks** for Las Vegas, and **30 impressions with no reported clicks** for North Las Vegas. These are organic search measurements, not appointments. The 20-city selection used observed impressions among already approved cities and retained the four previously fixed pages.
